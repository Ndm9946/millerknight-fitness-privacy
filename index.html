Miller Knight Fitness — Privacy Policy

Effective 2 October 2026

The short version

  • Everything you log stays in the app on your iPhone and Apple Watch. Your workout history can also be kept in your own iCloud account, and you can save an encrypted backup wherever you choose. The developer can't see any of it.
  • Health data the app reads from Apple Health is used only on your iPhone. It's never uploaded or shared — the only way it leaves your iPhone is inside an encrypted backup file you choose to make, which only your passphrase opens.
  • No analytics, no ads, no tracking, no third-party code and no servers. The developer collects no data.
  • You can export everything into an encrypted file, or delete everything, from Settings.
  • For adults (18+). Not a medical device.

Your workouts and iCloud

The app stores your training on your iPhone: your profile (name you choose, sex, birth year and month, height, experience), programs, gyms and equipment, every set you log (exercise, weight, reps, effort, time), your sessions, stretch routines and edits you make to the exercise library.

If you are signed in to iCloud, this training data is also kept in your own private iCloud database, so it comes back on a new iPhone. Your birth month and session notes are stored there encrypted. Only you can read your private iCloud data. The developer can't see it, and nobody else can. Apple stores it under Apple's privacy policy.

The app never puts anything from Apple Health in iCloud, nor your body measurements (weight, body fat, tape measurements, scan results), goals, food intake, readiness or photos. An automated test checks this on every build of the app's tests, run before every release: it fails if such a field ever appears in the data the app keeps in iCloud.

Apple Health

With your permission, the app reads these from Apple Health:

  • heart rate, resting heart rate and heart rate variability
  • respiratory rate, sleeping wrist temperature and blood oxygen
  • sleep
  • weight, body fat percentage and lean body mass

They are used only on your iPhone, to work out readiness, recovery and body trends. The app never uploads them, never shares them with anyone (including a leaderboard group), never uses them for advertising and never sells them. The only way they leave your iPhone is inside an encrypted backup file you choose to make (see Exporting your data), which only your passphrase opens.

The app writes only two things to Apple Health: your strength workouts, with their real start and end times, and your body weight, only when you type it in and choose to save it. It never writes estimates. It doesn't read steps, workouts from other apps, or any reproductive or clinical health records.

You can change or turn off this access at any time in the Health app (Sharing › Apps › Miller Knight Fitness) or in iPhone Settings › Privacy & Security › Health.

Body data, photos and Face ID

Body measurements, goals, food intake and calorie estimates, readiness and anything else about your body are kept in a separate store on your iPhone. That store is never synced to iCloud and is excluded from iCloud and computer backups. It leaves your iPhone only inside an encrypted backup file you choose to make.

Progress photos are taken with the app's own camera screen (the app never opens your photo library). Each photo is encrypted on your iPhone with a key kept in this iPhone's keychain and excluded from backups.

Face ID (or your passcode) is asked before the app shows your progress photos, the Me pane on the Body tab, your body measurements and intake under Progress, your goals, the body scan and the pregnancy and postpartum settings, and again every time you export, import or delete your data. The app locks them again a minute after you leave it. The readiness card on Home is shown without Face ID. Face ID is handled by iOS; the app never receives any face data.

Because body data and photos aren't in your backups, the encrypted export below is how you move them to a new iPhone.

The experimental body scan

The optional body-measurement scan uses the camera and depth sensor. Frames are processed on your iPhone and discarded straight away; only the resulting measurements are kept, in the on-device body store. The scan is clearly marked experimental and its numbers are estimates.

Pregnancy and postpartum mode

If you turn this mode on (where available), the stage you choose stays on your iPhone — the only way it leaves is inside an encrypted backup file you choose to make. It is never synced to iCloud, never shared with a leaderboard group and never written to Apple Health. It adjusts which exercises the app suggests; it is general fitness guidance, not medical advice.

Leaderboard groups

Leaderboards are optional and off until you create or join a group.

  • Who sees it: a group is a shared space in the group owner's iCloud account. Only people the owner adds (by their iCloud email address or phone number, at most 10 people including the owner, all 18 or older) can open it. The owner knows who they invited; other members see the pseudonym you choose.
  • What is shared: one row per day with daily totals only, and only the ones you turn on, each off until you choose: total weight lifted, gym minutes, whether you trained, and your streak. Plus your pseudonym, which totals you share, the app version and the day you joined. Never your workouts or sets, nothing from Apple Health, nothing about your body, no names or photos. Any number above zero shows you trained that day.
  • When it is sent: each day's totals are sent the first time the app runs unlocked after midnight; a later correction is sent the same way. iCloud keeps when each row was saved, which is the moment the app ran — not when you trained. The owner, as the iCloud account holding the group, can see those raw records.
  • Turning a total off removes it from every earlier day as well.
  • Leaving and closing: leaving a group deletes your rows from it. The owner can remove anyone and can close the group, which deletes it for everyone. Rows older than 400 days are deleted automatically. If you are offline, this finishes the next time the app is online.

Apple Watch and the Lock Screen

The Watch app keeps the latest plan your iPhone sent and its own copy of every workout and set you log on the Watch — it uses them to show what you lifted last time and to spot records. That copy stays on the Watch until you use Delete my data, which erases it. Sets on their way to your iPhone also wait in a small queue on the Watch; each one stays there for at least 30 days after your iPhone confirms it has it, and is removed the next time the Watch app starts after that. The Watch app talks only to your own iPhone and has no iCloud of its own.

During a workout the iPhone's Lock Screen can show your current set and heart rate; Settings › Hide numbers on lock screen hides the numbers there, and VoiceOver doesn't read them out while it's on. Rest-timer alerts are local notifications created on your iPhone.

Exporting your data

Settings › Data › Export encrypted backup puts your training history, your body data (including your readiness history, which holds the sleep, heart, breathing, wrist-temperature and blood-oxygen readings it was worked out from, and weights read from Apple Health), your pregnancy and postpartum settings and your progress photos into one .wkvault file, encrypted on your iPhone (AES-256-GCM, with a key made from a passphrase of at least 12 characters using PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with 600,000 rounds). You choose where the file goes — somewhere you control, for example Files on this iPhone or AirDrop to your Mac; the app never uploads it. The passphrase is never stored anywhere, so it can't be recovered — without it the file can't be opened. Import from backup restores it on a new iPhone. The app reminds you weekly to make a fresh backup; you can dismiss the reminder.

Settings › Data › Export training data (CSV) makes plain spreadsheet files of your training history only (no body data or photos). They are not encrypted, so share them with care.

Deleting your data

Settings › Privacy › Delete my data (after Face ID or your passcode, and two confirmations) deletes:

  • your training history on this iPhone and its copy in your iCloud;
  • your body data, progress photos and their encryption keys;
  • your leaderboard rows — you leave your group; if you own a group, it is closed for everyone and every member's shared days are deleted;
  • the copy on your Apple Watch (the next time it connects to this iPhone).

What stays: workouts and body weights the app saved to Apple Health stay in Health — delete them in the Health app if you want. What the app read from Apple Health (weight, sleep, heart data) stays in Health and is read again next time unless you turn off access. If this iPhone is offline or not signed in to iCloud, your iCloud copy is deleted the next time this iPhone is online and signed in to iCloud with the app open — don't delete the app before then. Deleting the app alone removes what is on the iPhone, but not your iCloud copy or your leaderboard rows, so use Delete my data first.

Diagnostics

Settings › Diagnostics shows counts and timings (for example how long logging a set takes, when iCloud last synced, free storage) so problems can be spotted. They stay on your iPhone and never include a health value, weight lifted, name or photo. You can export a diagnostics file and share it yourself if you want help; it holds the same counts, timings, dates and versions, except free storage, which is only shown on screen. Nothing is sent automatically.

What we don't do

  • No analytics, crash-reporting or advertising code, and no third-party code of any kind.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no ad identifiers.
  • No servers or accounts of our own: the developer receives none of your data.
  • No selling or sharing of data.

Apple's services

The app uses Apple's own services — iCloud, Apple Health, Face ID and, for testers, TestFlight — which Apple runs under its own privacy policy. If you test the app with TestFlight, Apple may pass crash reports and any feedback you send to the developer; they are used only to fix the app.

Adults only, and not medical advice

The app is for adults (18 or older). It is not a medical device. Readiness, sleep, body-fat and calorie numbers are estimates, and exercise suggestions are general fitness information. Check with a doctor before starting a new program and before making any medical decision, especially if you are pregnant, recently gave birth, or have a health condition.
